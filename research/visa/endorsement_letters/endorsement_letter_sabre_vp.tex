%% ── MARKET GAP ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{The market problem AMTTP solves is real and unsolved.}

In enterprise technology sales, I deal daily with the gap between what
compliance technology vendors promise and what their products actually
deliver.  I can state from direct commercial experience that the
blockchain compliance market has a fundamental structural deficiency:
\textit{every existing solution operates after transaction settlement}.

Chainalysis, Elliptic, and TRM~Labs---the three dominant vendors in this
space, collectively valued at over US\$10~billion---provide analytics and
alerting on blockchain transactions that have already been confirmed and
are therefore irreversible.  They are forensic tools.  They can tell you
that a suspicious transaction occurred; they cannot prevent it from
occurring.  This is not a minor limitation.  For financial institutions
subject to FCA, FinCEN, or FATF regulations, discovering \textit{after
the fact} that they have processed an illicit transaction creates
regulatory liability, reputational damage, and remediation costs that no
amount of post-hoc alerting can eliminate.

Olusegun's Anti-Money Laundering Transaction Trust Protocol (AMTTP) is,
to the best of my knowledge after {[X]}~years in enterprise technology,
the only system in existence that provides \textbf{pre-settlement
compliance enforcement} on blockchain transactions.  It intervenes
before finality: transactions are scored, classified (Approve / Review /
Escrow / Block), and gated at the protocol level within the block
confirmation window.  This is not an incremental improvement on existing
products.  It is a categorically different value proposition.
