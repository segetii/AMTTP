\textbf{1.\enspace AMTTP: pre-settlement compliance infrastructure}

My principal project is the Anti-Money Laundering Transaction Trust Protocol (AMTTP), a blockchain-native compliance system designed to make risk decisions before transaction finality. This is the central product insight behind the platform. Most existing compliance tools in digital assets operate after settlement, when funds have already moved and risk has already crystallised. AMTTP instead performs machine-learning risk scoring, graph-based relationship analysis, sanctions screening, and policy evaluation within the Ethereum confirmation window (approximately 12 seconds), allowing a transaction to be approved, reviewed, escrowed, or blocked before settlement.

I built AMTTP as a four-layer architecture: open-source TypeScript and Python SDKs; a compliance layer of 7 parallel microservices; a machine-learning pipeline trained on 2.64 million Ethereum transactions; and a blockchain layer of 18 Solidity smart contracts. The production ML system achieved ROC-AUC 0.9879, and the wider platform runs across 17 containerised services with API gateway security, cryptographic signing, rate limiting, and monitoring. I also developed a cross-platform Flutter application, a Next.js ``War Room'' dashboard, and wallet integrations for real-time user use.

\textbf{2.\enspace Privacy-preserving and cyber-secure design}

I developed zkNAF, a zero-knowledge compliance framework, to address the conflict between regulatory transparency and privacy. It enables sanctions verification without revealing identities, risk validation without exposing raw scores, and KYC confirmation without disclosing unnecessary personal data. This is important both commercially and technically: it aligns with GDPR-style data minimisation while improving cyber-security by reducing data exposure, strengthening auditability, and making compliance flows more tamper-resistant.

\textbf{3.\enspace Research foundation: Blind Spot Decomposition Theory}

Alongside the engineering work, I developed Blind Spot Decomposition Theory (BSDT), a machine-learning framework for identifying hidden risk factors that ordinary detectors miss. BSDT addresses a practical problem in fraud and anomaly detection: models often fail not because they are useless, but because important risk information is concealed, weakly represented, or structurally indirect. My framework provides a formal way to identify these blind spots and improve detection without retraining the underlying model. In validation, BSDT improved recall by up to 84.4 percentage points across multiple datasets and domains. This research now underpins the analytical logic of the AMTTP risk engine.

\textbf{4.\enspace Cross-domain system stability}

I extended the same computational framework beyond fraud detection into broader system-stability analysis. The same approach can be used for financial-system risk, blockchain transaction monitoring, infrastructure instability, and other complex dynamic systems. This matters because it shows that the work is not a one-off compliance tool, but a reusable computational method for early failure detection. For example, the framework identified structural instability in the ERCOT electricity crisis ahead of cascading failure, showing relevance to critical infrastructure as well as digital finance.

In commercial terms, this is a credible Compliance-as-a-Service product for enterprise fintech.
