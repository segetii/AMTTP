\section*{4. Blind-Spot Decomposition and the Geometry of System Collapse (Zenodo)}
\textbf{Published:} 16 March 2026 \quad $\vert$ \quad \textbf{Metrics:} 10 Views, 16 Downloads (as of 6 April 2026)\\
\textbf{DOI:} \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.19038783}\\
This third paper is, in my view, the strongest theoretical extension of the research programme. Rather than presenting BSDT only as a post-model fraud-analysis tool, it reformulates structural instability as a geometric and control problem. The paper shows that the BSDT energy functional is topologically equivalent to the interaction potential of a coupled dynamical system through Morse-index equivalence at critical points, two-sided gradient bounds, and homotopy equivalence of sublevel sets. It then derives a spectral phase-transition result: below a critical manifold, fixed coupling remains stable; above that manifold, no constant coupling is sufficient, and only adaptive friction achieves marginal stability.

That matters for this application because it demonstrates original mathematical work, not just implementation. The paper also connects the theory to concrete, non-trivial domains without retraining: FDIC banking data with a six-quarter lead before the 2008 GFC and AUROC 0.867 with zero false alarms in the documented benchmark window, Terra/Luna-style collapse dynamics with a 30-hour early alarm, and ERCOT Texas power-grid instability with AUC approaching 0.9996--0.9997 and a 72-hour lead before rolling blackouts. For Tech Nation purposes, I rely on this paper less for readership metrics and more as evidence that my work has evolved from product architecture into reusable theory with potential relevance to regulated finance and critical infrastructure.

\section*{5. Universal Detection Principle (UDP)}
\textbf{DOI:} \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.19037687}\\
My fourth paper, \textbf{Universal Detection Principle (UDP)}, extends the research programme beyond post-model blind-spot correction into a broader multi-operator anomaly-detection framework. UDP formalises a tensor-style representation built from multiple mathematical operator families and explores how structurally different operators can be composed to improve anomaly detection, separability, and interpretability across datasets.

This paper is newer than the first two publications and I therefore do not rely on it for the strongest traction claim in this application. Instead, I include it because it shows the upward trajectory of the research arc: from AMTTP system architecture, to BSDT failure decomposition, to a more general theory of instability and control, and then to UDP as a broader operator-based detection framework.
