G1. Discretisation Error (Open Problem P5)
G1.1 Euler scheme
Fix step size h > 0. The explicit Euler discretisation of the canonical ODE is
$$X_{t+h} = X_t + h \left[ F_{\mathrm{base}}(X_t) - g_X(X_t) - \gamma(X_t),\frac{\langle
F(X_t),, g_X(X_t)\rangle}{|g_X(X_t)|^2}, g_X(X_t) \right].$$
Proposition G1.1 (Euler energy increment). Under (A1)–(A4) and the angle condition
$\langle g_X, F_{\mathrm{base}}\rangle \leq |g_X|^2$, the one-step energy increment
satisfies
$$E(X_{t+h}) - E(X_t) \leq h,\dot{E}(X_t) + \tfrac{1}
{2}h^2,\Lambda(X_t),|X_t|^2_{\mathrm{op}},$$
where $\Lambda(X_t) := \lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2_X E(X_t))$ and $|\cdot|_{\mathrm{op}}$
is the operator norm of $\nabla^2_X E$.
Proof sketch. Taylor-expand $E(X_{t+h})$ to second order about $X_t$, using $\nabla_X E
= g_X$ (established in §5.3) and the Hessian bound of Proposition 7.6. The first-order term
recovers $h\dot{E}$; the second-order remainder is bounded by $\frac{h^2}
{2}\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2_X E)|\dot{X}|^2$, and $|\dot{X}|\leq |F_{\mathrm{base}}| +
(1+\gamma)|g_X|$ by the triangle inequality. $\square$
Corollary G1.2 (Step-size for monotone Euler). Energy is non-increasing at the discrete
step if
$$h \leq \frac{-2\dot{E}(X_t)}{\Lambda(X_t),|\dot{X}(X_t)|^2}.$$
=== PAGE 2 ===
Combined with the adaptive step rule $h_t = h_0/(1 + \lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2_X E(X_t)))$
from §H.1.4, monotonicity holds whenever $|\dot{X}|^2 \leq 2(1+\Lambda)/\Lambda \cdot (-
\dot{E}/h_0)$, which is satisfied on compact sublevel sets by the boundedness of $\dot{X}$
(Theorem 9.4).
Corollary G1.3 (ε-regularisation bias). For the regularised ODE with denominator
$|g_X|^2 + \varepsilon$, the energy derivative satisfies
$$\dot{E}_\varepsilon = \dot{E} + \gamma,\frac{\varepsilon,\langle F, g_X\rangle}
{|g_X|^2(|g_X|^2 + \varepsilon)}.$$
The bias term is $O(\varepsilon / |g_X|^2)$ for $\varepsilon \ll |g_X|^2$, and vanishes as
$\varepsilon \to 0$. On sublevel sets where $|g_X|^2 \geq \delta > 0$ the bias is uniformly
$O(\varepsilon/\delta)$, so the regularised system satisfies Ė_ε ≤ 0 provided $\varepsilon <
\delta \cdot |g_X|^2 / |\langle F, g_X\rangle|$. Practical rule: choose $\varepsilon = 10^{-8}
\cdot \operatorname{median}(|g_X|^2)$ from a warm-up run.
G1.2 Runge–Kutta (RK4)
RK4 has local truncation error $O(h^5)$ and global error $O(h^4)$. The energy
monotonicity condition weakens to requiring $h < h^(X_t)$ where $h^$ is the same bound
as Corollary G1.2 but with the coefficient $\frac{1}{2}$ replaced by $\frac{1}{120}$ (from the
RK4 error constant). In practice, RK4 with the adaptive step rule of §H.1.4 is unconditionally
safe on compact sublevel sets. For stiff problems (large $\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2_X E)$),
implicit midpoint is preferred: it is symplectic and energy-non-increasing by construction.
G2. Stochastic Extension (Open Problem P6)
G2.1 Canonical SDE
Define the canonical SDE
$$dX = H(X),dt + \Sigma(X),dW_t,$$
where $H(X)$ is the canonical-ODE drift (Section 4) and $W_t$ is standard $n$-
dimensional Brownian motion.
Theorem G2.1 (Itô energy equation). Under (A1)–(A4) and $\Sigma \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^n,
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m})$ with locally bounded operator norm,
$$dE = \langle g_X, H\rangle,dt + \langle g_X, \Sigma,dW_t\rangle + \tfrac{1}
{2}\operatorname{tr}!\left(\Sigma^T \nabla^2_X E,\Sigma\right)dt.$$
The expected energy dynamics are
=== PAGE 3 ===
$$\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[E] = (1-\gamma)\bigl[\langle g_X, F_{\mathrm{base}}\rangle -
|g_X|^2\bigr] + \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}!\left(\Sigma^T \nabla^2_X E,\Sigma\right).$$
Proof. Apply Itô’s lemma to $E(X_t)$ with $f = E \in C^2$: $dE = (\nabla_X E)^T dX +
\frac{1}{2}(dX)^T \nabla^2_X E,(dX)$. Since $\nabla_X E = g_X$ (§5.3) and $(dX)^T
\nabla^2_X E,(dX) = \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^T \nabla^2_X E,\Sigma),dt$ in the Itô sense,
the result follows by substituting the canonical ODE for the drift and using Lemma 7.7.
$\square$
G2.2 Fisher-optimal noise
Proposition G2.2 (Fisher noise and chart-independence). Under the Fisher choice $G(X)
= I_S(S(X))$ and noise covariance $\Sigma(X) = I_X^\dagger{}^{1/2}$ (Moore-Penrose
square root of the state Fisher metric), the Itô correction on the target set ${S = 0}$
reduces to
$$\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}!\left(\Sigma^T \nabla^2_X E,\Sigma\right)\Big|_{{S=0}} =
\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}(I_X),$$
which is chart-independent (an integer) and equals the dimension of the identifiable tangent
subspace.
Proof. On ${S=0}$, $\eta = 2GS = 0$, so $\nabla^2_X E = 2J^T I_S J = 2I_X$ (§K.3). Then
$\operatorname{tr}(I_X^{\dagger/2} \cdot 2I_X \cdot I_X^{\dagger/2}) = 2\operatorname{tr}
(I_X^{\dagger/2} I_X I_X^{\dagger/2}) = 2\operatorname{rank}(I_X)$, using the identity
$I_X^{\dagger/2} I_X I_X^{\dagger/2} = P_{\mathrm{range}(I_X)}$. Chart-independence
follows from Theorem I.1: $\operatorname{rank}(I_X)$ is invariant under the transformation
$\tilde{I}_X = T^T I_X T$. $\square$
Corollary G2.3 (Free-energy identification). Define the free energy $\mathcal{F} =
\mathbb{E}[E] - \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{rank}(I_X) \cdot t$. Under the Fisher noise,
$\mathcal{F}$ is non-increasing along $\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}[E]$ whenever the
deterministic descent condition of Theorem 9.1 holds. The Itô correction is therefore a
constant-rate drift, not an instability source.
G2.3 Stochastic ultimate boundedness
Theorem G2.4 (Stochastic ultimate boundedness). Let $c_\Sigma := \frac{1}{2}\sup_X
\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^T \nabla^2_X E,\Sigma)$. Under the hypotheses of Theorem 9.4
with admissibility condition $M + c_\Sigma < \Psi^*$, there exist $0 < R_- \leq R_+$ such
that $E(X_t)$ is ultimately bounded in $[R_-, R_+]$ almost surely.
Proof. The Itô correction enters as an additional effective disturbance of amplitude
$c_\Sigma$. Replace $M$ by $M_{\mathrm{eff}} = M + c_\Sigma$ in the proof of Theorem

9.4. The unimodal structure of $\Psi$ is unchanged; the roots $R_\pm$ shift to absorb the
noise term. Since $c_\Sigma$ is bounded on compact sublevel sets (by (A1) and
boundedness of $\Sigma$), the argument carries through. $\square$
Practical guidance: For BSDT applications, the Itô correction is $c_\Sigma = \frac{1}
{2}\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_0^{-1} \cdot \Sigma_{\mathrm{noise}})$ where $\Sigma_0$ is
the calibration covariance and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{noise}}$ is the measurement noise
covariance. The adjusted admissibility threshold is $\Psi^_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Psi^ -
c_\Sigma$.
G3. Time-Varying G(t) and Updated Exponential Rate (Open
Problem P7)
Theorem G3.1 (Exponential rate under metric flow). Under the hypotheses of Theorem
F.2 with $F_{\mathrm{base}} \equiv 0$, constant $G^$, and the relaxation schedule
$\dot{G} = \sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}(G^ - G)$, the exponential decay rate of $E$ under the
coupled (X, G) dynamics is
$$E(X(t)) \leq E(X_0),e^{-\rho_{\mathrm{flow}}, t},\quad \rho_{\mathrm{flow}} = \rho_F \cdot
\frac{\mu_(t)}{\mu_(t) + \delta_G(t)},$$
where $\rho_F = 4\mu_I^2 \sigma_{\min}^2(J)(1-\gamma_{\max})/M_I$ is the frozen-metric
rate of Theorem J.1, $\mu_(t) = \lambda_{\min}(G(t))$, and $\delta_G(t) = |G(t) - G^|_F /
|G^*|_F$ is the relative metric error at time $t$.
Proof sketch. From Theorem F.2, $V(X,G) = E + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}^{-1}|G-
G^|_F^2$ is non-increasing. The metric-error term satisfies $|G(t) - G^|F \leq |G(0) - G^*|F
e^{-\sigma{\mathrm{rel}} t}$ (linear relaxation ODE). Substituting into the energy-descent
inequality, $\dot{E} \leq -(1-\gamma)|g_X|^2 \cdot \mu(t)/\mu_(t)+\delta_G(t))$ and
applying the Grönwall argument of Theorem 9.3 with the time-varying coefficient gives the
displayed rate. $\square$
Remark G3.2 (Rate monotonicity). As $t \to \infty$, $G(t) \to G^*$, $\delta_G(t) \to 0$,
and $\rho_{\mathrm{flow}}(t) \to \rho_F$. The metric-flow system therefore converges to
the frozen-metric rate from below. The speed of this convergence is controlled by
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$: large $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ collapses the gap rapidly at the
cost of larger transient metric oscillations.
Corollary G3.3 (Optimal relaxation rate). The integrated energy bound $\int_0^\infty
E(t),dt$ is minimised at $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}^* = \rho_F \cdot \mu_(0) / |G(0) - G^|_F$,
balancing fast metric convergence against energy overshoot during the transient.

G4. Rank Condition: Quantitative Near-Singularity Bounds
G4.1 The gap
Theorem 9.3 requires $\sigma_{\min}(J(X)) \geq \sigma > 0$ on the compact sublevel set.
Near kinematic singularities (robotics) or degenerate calibrations (BSDT), this fails. The
regularised ODE ($\varepsilon > 0$) is prescribed but without quantifying the convergence
degradation.
G4.2 Degraded-rank convergence theorem
Theorem G4.1 (Near-singular convergence). Let $\sigma_{\min}(J(X)) \geq \sigma(X) >
0$ on ${E \leq c}$ but allow $\sigma(X) \to 0$ near the singular set $\mathcal{S}$. Define
the effective rate
$$\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(X) = \frac{4\sigma(X)^2 \mu_G^2}{M_G}(1-\gamma(X)).$$
Then for the regularised ODE ($\varepsilon > 0$), $E(X(t)) \leq E(X_0)\exp(-\int_0^t
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(X(s)),ds)$.
Proof. From the proof of Theorem 9.3, $|g_X|^2 \geq 4\sigma(X)^2 \mu_G^2 / M_G \cdot
E$, using the pointwise singular value $\sigma(X)$ in place of the uniform $\sigma$.
Grönwall’s inequality with the time-varying coefficient gives the exponential bound with the
integrated rate. $\square$
G4.3 Singularity avoidance condition
Proposition G4.2. The canonical ODE avoids $\mathcal{S}$ along trajectories starting in
${E \leq c}$ if
$$\inf_{X \in {E \leq c}} \sigma_{\min}(J(X)) \geq \sigma_{\min}^{\mathrm{crit}} :=
\sqrt{\frac{M_G,M}{4\mu_G^2(1-\gamma_{\max})}},$$
where $M$ is the disturbance bound of Theorem 9.4. Below this threshold, the gradient
$g_X$ is too weak to prevent $F_{\mathrm{base}}$ from driving the trajectory toward
$\mathcal{S}$.
Operational check: Before running the canonical ODE on a new domain, compute
$\sigma_{\min}(J(X_0))$ and compare to $\sigma_{\min}^{\mathrm{crit}}$. For BSDT with
$J = I$ (affine $S$), this condition is automatic ($\sigma_{\min} = 1$). For task-space
robotics, compute the minimum manipulability $w(q) = \sqrt{\det(J(q)J(q)^T)}$ over the
trajectory and flag if $w < \sigma_{\min}^{\mathrm{crit}}$.

G5. False-Positive Rate: ERCOT Early-Warning Validation
Protocol
G5.1 The gap
The ERCOT result (+1033 h lead from hour 23) gives no false-positive denominator. Without
a false-positive rate, the lead time statistic is uninterpretable as a detection result.
G5.2 Proposed validation protocol
Definition G5.1 (Canonical alarm). An alarm fires at time $t$ when $E(t) > \mu_{1:t-1} +
2\sigma_{1:t-1}$ for $t \geq 20$ (as defined in §1.4 of the report). A true positive (TP) is an
alarm within $[-L_{\max}, 0]$ steps of a labelled crisis onset. A false positive (FP) is any
alarm outside all crisis windows.
Protocol G5.2 (ERCOT FPR estimation):
1. Run the canonical alarm on the full 2019–2022 ERCOT series (T = 8,760 h), using only
non-crisis hours to define the background distribution.
2. Label crisis windows: Winter Uri (hours 1,040–1,200), plus any NERC-declared
emergency events in the data.
3. Count FP alarms per 1,000 non-crisis hours. Denote this $\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}}$.
4. Compute the detection precision at lead $L$: $\mathrm{Precision}(L) = \mathrm{TP}(L) /
(\mathrm{TP}(L) + \mathrm{FP})$.
5. Report the lead-precision frontier: the curve $(\mathrm{lead,time},,
\mathrm{precision})$ for $L \in [0, 1033]$ h.
Proposed benchmark thresholds for ERCOT:
$\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}} < 5$ per 1,000 h (i.e., < 0.5% false alarm rate) is deployment-
grade for grid operators.
$\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}} < 20$ per 1,000 h is research-grade, consistent with existing
grid stress literature.
Expected result: The BSDT camouflage channel (δ_C) alarm at hour 23 is from solar
volatility exceeding its 2σ envelope — a condition likely to recur during routine high-
variability periods (summer afternoons, storm fronts). The FPR is expected to be non-
negligible. The question is whether it falls within the benchmark range.
G5.3 Generalised FPR formula
=== PAGE 7 ===
For any domain with background rate $\lambda_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and alarm threshold $\mu +
k\sigma$:
$$\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{bg}} \cdot 2(1 - \Phi(k)),$$
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. For $k = 2$ (as used in the report),
$\lambda_{\mathrm{FP}} \approx 0.046 \cdot \lambda_{\mathrm{bg}}$. This means roughly
1 false alarm per 22 time steps under normality. The expanding-window estimator of $\mu$
and $\sigma$ reduces the effective false-positive rate below this floor, but by how much
depends on the autocorrelation of $E(t)$ — which must be measured per domain.
G6. Fisher Tracking Schedule: Practitioner Guidance on Chart
Dependence
G6.1 The gap
Remark I.2 states that the locked canonical ODE is chart-dependent under the tracking
schedule (F1). No guidance is given on when this matters in practice or how to detect it.
G6.2 Chart-dependence severity metric
Definition G6.1 (Reparametrisation sensitivity). For a diffeomorphism $X =
\Phi(\tilde{X})$ with Jacobian $T = \partial\Phi/\partial\tilde{X}$, the reparametrisation
sensitivity of the canonical trajectory is
$$\kappa_{\mathrm{rep}}(X) := \frac{|T^{-1}H(X) - H(\Phi^{-1}(X))|}{|H(X)|},$$
where $H(X)$ is the canonical-ODE drift. For the Euclidean canonical ODE,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{rep}} = |I - T^{-1}|_{\mathrm{op}} + O(|T - I|^2)$ near the identity.
Proposition G6.2. Chart dependence is negligible (i.e., $\kappa_{\mathrm{rep}} < \delta$
for a tolerance $\delta$) whenever $|T - I|_{\mathrm{op}} < \delta/2$, i.e., the Jacobian of
the reparametrisation is close to the identity. This holds whenever the domain coordinates
are approximately Cartesian in the operating region.

G7. Admissibility Threshold Sensitivity to Calibration
Conditioning
G7.1 The gap
The admissibility threshold $\Psi^* = \sigma\theta\mu_G / \sqrt{M_G}$ depends on
$\mu_G$ and $M_G$, the eigenvalue bounds of $G$. For BSDT, $G = \Sigma_0^{-1}$, so
$\mu_G = 1/\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0)$ and $M_G = 1/\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_0)$. The
dependence on $\kappa(\Sigma_0) = \lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ is not explicit.
G7.2 Explicit conditioning bound
Proposition G7.1 (BSDT admissibility and calibration conditioning). For $G =
\Sigma_0^{-1}$,
$$\Psi^* = \frac{\sigma,\theta}{\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0),\sqrt{1/\lambda_{\min}
(\Sigma_0)}} = \frac{\sigma,\theta,\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_0)}}{\lambda_{\max}
(\Sigma_0)} = \frac{\sigma,\theta}{\kappa(\Sigma_0)^{1/2},\lambda_{\max}^{1/2}
(\Sigma_0)}.$$
Equivalently, $\Psi^* = \sigma\theta / \sqrt{\kappa(\Sigma_0) \cdot \lambda_{\max}
(\Sigma_0)}$.
Corollary G7.2. The admissibility threshold degrades as $1/\sqrt{\kappa(\Sigma_0)}$. A 10×
increase in calibration conditioning shrinks the safe disturbance budget by $\sqrt{10}
